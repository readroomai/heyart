## Long description

You cannot see your own work for the first time twice. Everyone else can — and they form an opinion
about your brand from a crop, a colour and a hierarchy long before they read a single word you
wrote.

HiArt gives you that first look back.

Upload a visual, tell it where the work will be published, who it is for, what you want it to
achieve and how it should feel. In under a minute you get a structured creative review: the order a
viewer's eye is likely to move in, what the piece appears to communicate, nine scored dimensions
(attention, clarity, hierarchy, trust, professionalism, brand fit, originality, platform fit,
readability), how different audiences are likely to read the same frame, what is working, what is
weakening it, where it might be misunderstood, accessibility concerns, and five prioritised changes
ordered by impact — each with why, how, and what it should achieve.

It ends with a revision brief and a ready-to-paste creative prompt you can hand straight to a
designer or an image tool.

Three modes:

- **Visual Review** — one visual, read the way an audience reads it.
- **A/B Compare** — two variants, one clear recommendation, and an honest statement of what you give
  up by choosing it.
- **Feed Audit** — a screenshot of your profile, feed, gallery or portfolio, assessed as one body of
  work: what it says, what stays unclear, where it contradicts itself, plus three visual directions
  and a seven-item checklist.

Save a Brand Profile — your audience, personality, colours, and the words your brand should never be
described as — and brand fit gets judged against your actual identity rather than a generic idea of
good design.

HiArt does not replace designers, generate images, or connect to your social accounts. It is a
second opinion before the work goes public.

## What makes it different

1. **It answers one question well.** Not a creative suite — one interaction: upload, brief, report.
   A first-time user finishes in under two minutes.
2. **The brief changes the answer.** The same image reviewed for "stop the scroll on TikTok, young
   audience" and "build trust with investors on a website" produces genuinely different reports.
3. **It names the trade-off.** Every recommendation costs something. HiArt says what.
4. **It refuses to flatten your work.** The model is explicitly instructed not to default to
   minimalism and not to strip personality in order to raise a professionalism score.
5. **It will not judge people.** When a person appears, HiArt comments on framing, lighting, styling
   and composition. It never identifies anyone, rates appearance, or infers sensitive attributes.
6. **It refuses to lie about certainty.** No engagement predictions, no conversion promises. Every
   report lists the assumptions the model made and its confidence.
7. **A/B Compare is allowed to say "too close to call."**

## Core features

- Visual Review, A/B Compare and Feed Audit
- Visual Score 0–100 with a plain-language band, so meaning never depends on colour alone
- Attention path — first, second and third thing noticed, with reasons
- Nine scored dimensions, each with its reasoning
- Audience reads: the interpretation, the positive signal and the concern, per audience
- Five prioritised improvements with why / how / expected effect
- Things to preserve — what not to touch
- Accessibility and platform-specific observations
- Revision brief plus a copyable creative revision prompt
- Brand Profiles, including the words your brand should never be
- Custom audiences (familiarity, knowledge, existing sentiment, what they care about)
- History with search, mode filters, favourites, rename, duplicate setup and delete
- Revocable public share links, image hidden by default
- Free beta: three reviews a day, no card required

## Suggested follow-up reply

Good question — here's the honest boundary.

HiArt is a model's opinion, not a measurement. It has never met your audience and can't run a test,
so it will never tell you a design will perform better. What it's genuinely good at is catching the
gap between what you meant and what a stranger sees in the first second — the announcement that
isn't legible, the crop that will eat your lower third, the two typographic systems fighting each
other across a feed.

Where it's weakest: it can misread small text and unusual layouts, and like most models trained on a
lot of design work it has a mild bias toward convention. We instruct it not to default to
minimalism, but if it ever tells you to remove the thing that makes the work yours — disagree with
it. That's a case where you're right and it isn't.

Every report lists its assumptions and a confidence figure for exactly that reason.

## AI limitations note

HiArt produces an AI-assisted assessment of how a visual may be perceived. It is not a measurement
and not a prediction of audience behaviour. It cannot promise engagement, reach or conversion.
Vision models misread small text, unusual layouts and context outside the frame, and two runs on the
same image can differ. Every report lists the assumptions made and a confidence figure. HiArt never
identifies people, rates appearance, or infers race, religion, sexuality, health or political
affiliation. Full detail at `/ai-limitations`.

## Privacy note

Uploads go to private storage and analyses are private by default. Sharing is an explicit action —
the original image stays hidden on a shared report until a visitor chooses to reveal it, and links
are revocable at any time. Shared reports never expose email addresses, private context, storage
paths or internal prompts. Images and prompts are never written to application logs. Your visual is
processed by an external AI provider (Google Gemini) to generate the review; during the free beta we
ask users not to upload confidential or legally sensitive material, and we do not claim zero
retention by that provider because we cannot guarantee it. Full detail at `/privacy`.
